AiO VPS · All-in-One Vote Printing System

Every vote, printed on paper the voter can see — and confirm.

AiO VPS is a touch-screen voting kiosk that turns each vote into a physical printed record. The voter watches their own ballot print behind a smart-glass window, checks that it matches their choice, and only then is it sealed as VOTED with a unique barcode. The paper roll becomes evidence that can be audited independently of any software.

The problem

Voters are asked to trust what they cannot see.

Elections only work when people believe the result. Today's options force a trade-off between speed and verifiability.

01

Screens ask for blind trust

On a screen-only voting machine the voter presses a button and sees a light or a beep. What was actually recorded inside the machine is invisible to them.

02

Paper ballots are slow and error-prone

Hand-marked ballots take a long time to count, and unclear or invalid marks can spoil a voter's intent.

03

Disputes need independent evidence

When a result is questioned, there must be a record that does not depend on the machine's own software, one that people can physically inspect.

The solution

One machine. The ease of a touch screen, the proof of paper.

AiO VPS combines a simple touch-screen ballot with a printed record that the voter personally verifies. Nothing is counted on faith: the voter sees it, confirms it, and the paper proves it.

See it

The chosen symbol is printed on paper. A smart-glass window turns clear and a light switches on so the voter can see the actual print, not a picture of it.

Confirm it

The voter compares the paper with their choice and answers Yes or No. The ballot is only sealed as VOTED once the printed paper has been checked.

Prove it

VOTED or REJECTED is printed on the same paper, accepted ballots get a unique barcode, and the roll is wound into a sealed cassette for audit.

Voting flow

From a single tap to a sealed paper ballot.

Seven guided steps. Every screen has a countdown, so the machine never stays stuck halfway through a vote.

  1. 1

    Start

    The voter taps Click here to vote. The machine starts from a known safe state: print windows opaque, print light off.

  2. 2

    Constituency check

    "Is your constituency XYZ?" A voter at the wrong booth is stopped before choosing anything and sent to the polling officer.

  3. 3

    Choose a symbol

    Large symbols in a simple scrolling grid, readable at a glance and usable whatever the voter's literacy level.

  4. 4

    Confirm the choice

    An enlarged preview of the selected symbol. No returns to the grid, so a mis-tap is harmless.

  5. 5

    Print

    A printer is selected, its smart-glass window turns transparent, the window light comes on, and the symbol is printed and fed up into view.

  6. 6

    Verify the paper

    The voter compares the printed paper with their choice and confirms it within a time limit.

  7. 7

    Seal the result

    The outcome is printed on the same paper, the roll moves on into the cassette, and the window turns opaque again for the next voter.

    ✓ VOTED + unique barcode ✕ REJECTED, voter sent to the officer
Safeguard

What if the print is wrong?

Because the voter checks real paper, a wrong print cannot slip through unnoticed. If the printed symbol does not match the choice, the ballot is marked REJECTED on the paper itself. A spoilt ballot can never be quietly counted, and the voter is directed to the polling officer.

The rejected print stays on the roll, so every problem leaves a visible, auditable trace instead of disappearing.

The live demo deliberately misprints some ballots so you can watch this safeguard work.

Inside the machine

Affordable, proven components working as one.

Touch display

480 × 800 portrait touch screen running a locked, full-screen voting app.

Dual thermal printers

Two independent 58 mm printers, used in a fixed order or chosen at random for each vote.

Smart-glass windows

PDLC glass stays opaque for privacy and turns clear only while the voter's own ballot is printing.

Motorised paper roll

Motors feed the paper upward into a single re-rolled cassette. Ballots are never loose.

Status lights

System and print lights show the polling officer exactly what the machine is doing.

Raspberry Pi controller

Drives every printer, motor, light and glass panel, and keeps a local registry of issued barcodes.

Some machines add a paper-trail viewer that shows a slip briefly behind glass. AiO VPS goes further: the voter must actively confirm the printed paper, a wrong print is marked REJECTED on that same paper, and every accepted ballot carries its own unique barcode.

Security by design

Built to resist tampering, and to expose it.

Security does not rest on a single lock. Each layer below makes an attack harder, and the paper record makes any attack visible.

No remote attack surface

Printing, barcode generation and storage all happen inside the machine, so it can run with every network interface switched off. There is nothing to reach over the internet.

Paper is the source of truth

Software cannot change paper the voter has already verified, so the roll can always be counted by hand to check any reported result.

The voter is the final check

Every ballot is verified by the one person who knows the intended vote, at the moment it is printed.

Unique barcodes

Each accepted ballot gets a one-time EAN-13 code from the machine's registry, so duplicated or forged slips stand out at reconciliation.

Locked-down kiosk

The app starts as a system service in full-screen mode. Power and settings are passcode-protected, and the voter never reaches a desktop or keyboard.

Sealed paper cassette

The roll is re-wound into a single cassette. Ballots are never handled loose, so none can be added or removed unnoticed.

A note on "unhackable".

No electronic system can honestly promise that it can never be attacked. AiO VPS offers a stronger and checkable guarantee: altering the software cannot alter the paper the voter has already verified, so tampering can be detected by auditing the roll.

Under the hood

Technical architecture

A single-file Tkinter kiosk (main.py) on a Raspberry Pi drives two thermal printers, two DC/servo motors, two PDLC smart-glass panels and the status LEDs over the GPIO header.

Hardware stack

  • ControllerRaspberry Pi (BCM GPIO)
  • Display480 × 800 portrait touch
  • Printers2 × 58 mm thermal, USB lp0/lp1
  • MotorsDC (default) / Servo / Both
  • Print windows2 × PDLC smart glass
  • LEDs2 window LEDs + green/red status
  • StorageSQLite (unique 12-digit barcodes)

Print sequence

  1. Printer chosen (fixed or random, PRINTER_CONFIG).
  2. Red LED on, green LED off.
  3. Smart glass of that window turns transparent.
  4. Window LED lights the ballot area.
  5. Motor feeds the paper while the symbol prints.
  6. Motor stops after motor_delay_time_*.
  7. Second pass prints VOTED + barcode, or REJECTED.
  8. Back to Home: glass and LEDs off.

Runs as a systemd service (install/vpm.service) that starts on boot, logs to syslog and restarts on crash. The service user must be in the lp group for printer access.
